1. The paper in one paragraph

The authors pooled every usable study that applied the Theory of Planned Behaviour (TPB) to a risky driving behaviour. They asked two things: how much TPB explains drivers' intentions and their actual behaviour, and which TPB factor matters most. Answer: TPB explains about a third to a half of the variance in both. Attitude is the strongest driver of intention, and intention is the strongest predictor of behaviour. It is the most recent and most complete evidence that TPB works for driving, which is why the deck cites it.

3. How to interpret the numbers

  • "Explains 30–51% of intention." In a regression or SEM, R² for intention is between .30 and .51 depending on the behaviour. In social science that is large. The rest is due to factors outside the model (personality, habit, situation) and measurement error.
  • "Observed and/or prospective behaviour." The behaviour was observed (e.g. in a simulator or on the road) or measured later than the questionnaire, not just reported at the same moment. This matters: it shows TPB predicts what drivers actually do next, which is the link to OBD-measured behaviour.
  • Compare with Ajzen (1991). Ajzen's general review averaged R ≈ .51 for behaviour (≈ 26% variance). Somoray finds 36–48% for driving behaviours, the same order of magnitude or better.
  • Attitude strongest. For a feedback system this is the most useful finding. Changing what drivers believe about the consequences of risky driving is the most promising lever.

4. Checklist while you read the full paper

Fill these in as you read. They are what you will cite in the literature-review chapter.

  1. Inclusion criteria: which designs, which populations (young / novice / professional drivers), which languages.
  2. The full list of behaviours, and the number of samples (k) for each.
  3. The pooled correlation matrix and path coefficients (usually a table plus a path diagram). Copy the β for each arrow, per behaviour if reported.
  4. R² for intention and for behaviour per behaviour: which behaviour sits at 30% and which at 51%.
  5. Heterogeneity statistics (I², Q) and whether moderators explain it. In particular, is prediction weaker for observed than for self-reported behaviour?
  6. Publication-bias tests (funnel plot, Egger's test, fail-safe N).
  7. Any extended-TPB variables they examined (past behaviour, descriptive norms, risk perception, moral norms).
  8. Limitations and future-research recommendations, especially any call for objective behaviour measures (telematics / naturalistic data). If it is there, quote it: it justifies your project directly.

Attitude strongest for intentionThe Coach / gamified feedback should mainly target outcome beliefs (attitude), and use peer comparison (norm) and achievable challenges (PBC) as secondary levers.
Intention strongest for behaviourCommitment devices ("pledge: no harsh braking this week") are a TPB-consistent gamification mechanic, because they act on intention.
Behaviour measures in most studies are self-reported or simulated (check in paper)Our contribution: an objective, continuous behaviour measure from OBD that TPB studies could use in place of self-report.
One sentence for the slides. "A 2024 meta-analysis of 94 samples (Somoray et al.) shows TPB explains 30–51% of drivers' intentions and 36–48% of observed or later behaviour, with attitude as the strongest lever. We use it as the psychological basis for what the system measures and how its feedback is designed."

6. Questions the doctor may ask

QuestionShort answer
Why a meta-analysis instead of one study?It pools 94 samples, so the effect sizes are far more reliable than any single study and it shows how they vary across behaviours.
Is 36–48% "good"?Yes for behavioural science. It also leaves more than half unexplained, which is room for habit and situational factors that OBD data can capture.
Does it prove TPB causes behaviour?No. Most pooled studies are correlational or prospective, not experimental. It shows prediction, not causation. Check whether the authors say this in their limitations.
